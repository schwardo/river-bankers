River Bankers — Release Notes

Changelog · newest version first

Version v0.4 (September 2026)

A small, focused pass on interaction compensation, prompted by a September playtest in which a Portage worker-steal was both the best play of the game and its one feels-bad moment. The attacks all remain legal and exactly as strong to make — but their victims are no longer left empty-handed. Confirmed balance-neutral in 15,000-game simulations at 3 and 4 players.

Why: Portage's swap invalidated an opponent's two-turn plan with zero counterplay and zero compensation — unlike Tribute Stone, whose forced recall at least pays its victim 3🐟. The fix extends Tribute Stone's compensation shape across the interaction suite, scaled to how much each attack actually takes.

Card changes before → after

  • Portage, compensated. Old: “…swap one of your workers on a river card with another worker on a different river card. Pay the source card’s per-item cost in 🐟.” New: the swap now names “another player’s worker” (swapping two of your own workers was always a no-op), and adds: “They slide back 2🐟 in compensation.” A forced exchange hurts less than Tribute Stone’s removal, so it pays less than 3. Printed VP stays 6★.
  • Tribute Stone & Snare Set, rescaled. Old: the recall victim slides back a flat 3🐟. New: they slide back the recalled card’s current per-item cost (2🐟 at River 1 up to 5🐟 at River 4) — deeper steals pay their victims more, matching the refund to what was actually taken.
  • New card: Basking Rocks (Stones · 7 icons, replacing the plain Boulder Field). “The first worker you spend from this card each turn is worth 1 item per player with workers on this card.” The deck’s first co-opetition card — everyone aboard gets the bonus on their own first spend, so company is welcome. 15,000-game simulations at 2, 3, and 4 players show the minted bonus is almost perfectly count-invariant (≈0.7 extra items per player per game) with no distortion of who wins.
  • Mud Wallow: friendly ties. Old: “…the player with the most workers moves back 2 spaces on the fish track. Ties: nobody.” New: “Ties: everyone tied.” A 4-icon card at three players splits 2–2 naturally, so the bonus was a dead letter far too often — it whiffed on a tie in both September playtests, and simulation puts the tie rate at 25% of resolutions. Paying every tied leader repairs the whiff without distorting the race (awardee win-rate edge moves +4.3 → +5.4 points, jams and game length unchanged). Cattail Cluster keeps “Ties: nobody”: it is a 4-player-only card, and at four players ties run 44% of resolutions — friendly ties there would pay about two winners per card and turn the most-workers race into a participation prize. The two cards now state different tie clauses on purpose; read each card.
  • Reed Walkway, capped. Old: “End of game: +2 VP per built structure of yours that uses Reeds.” New: adds “(max +8)”, matching Stone Causeway. Reeds are the deck’s widest structure band with a species discount behind them, so the reed engine was the only one whose ceiling was both uncapped and cheap to feed — it decided a September playtest at exactly +8, so in practice the cap trims only the runaway tail. Vine Trellis stays uncapped: vines have no discount and the thinnest demand, so its ceiling is the vine line’s reward for swimming upstream.
  • Cattail Marsh, clarified. Old: “When you build: each Reed worker counts as 2 reeds.” New: “When you build another structure: …” No rules change — effects never apply to the build that creates them — but playtesters kept reading the discount as applying to Cattail Marsh itself and planning builds they couldn’t afford. The card now says what the rulebook always meant.
  • Old Growth’s ×2 survives the Shoreline. Old: “If this card is at River 3 or 4, each worker you retrieve yields 2 Logs instead of 1.” New: “If this card is at River 3 or 4 or on the Shoreline…” Previously, covering Old Growth’s last icon retired it to the Shoreline and switched its own multiplier off — so winning an icon could leave your Logs total unchanged or even lower it. Simulation showed this trap fired in roughly 30% of 4-player games and left the card’s investors two win-rate points under fair share; with the ×2 persisting they return to fair share, with no measurable effect on anyone else. This has no impact on end-game scoring: the bonus applies only to workers you retrieve during play to pay build costs — workers still on the card at game end count singly, exactly as before.
  • New card: Twig Bridge (4 Vines · 3🐟 · 5★). “Headwaters auctions you trigger cost 2🐟, whatever the slot.” Vines was the deck’s acknowledged soft spot: second-lowest demand, the only material with no species discount, and most of its pull locked in engine cards that print 0★ on their own. A vines commitment had no floor when the engine failed to come together. Twig Bridge is a pure-vines monument that pays outright, so the commitment now has one. Its effect spans the Headwaters trigger gradient — normally 2/3/4🐟 by distance — flattening it to the nearest-slot rate. Buy it for the 5★: at 10,000 games per condition the discount’s contribution is not statistically distinguishable from zero, because the card tends to land late and its owner averages under one Headwaters auction afterwards. Net VP lands at +0.14 / +0.67 / +0.62 at 2, 3 and 4 players — inside the ±1 band at every count. The structure deck goes from 49 cards to 50.

Version v0.3 (July 2026)

The first balance pass driven by Alpha playtesting on BoardGameArena. With the game live in private Alpha, real turns surfaced a starter card that was quietly far too strong — and a rule fix, confirmed in simulation, brings it back into line. This version also adds a first-mover advantage to auction resolution so that a full jam no longer leaves the player who opened the card with nothing.

Why: the BGA implementation let Channel Clearer be used repeatedly each turn — a muskrat could clear every opponent Reed worker off the river, every turn, for free. It felt mean and dominant in Alpha, and the simulator agreed: forcing the card into play pushed the muskrat win-rate +6 points above its fair share (31% at 4-player where 25% is even; 39% at 3-player where 33% is even) — well outside the ±5-point balance band.

Rule changes

  • First-mover advantage. When an auction is so heavily overbid that no one would win a single item (no bid exceeds the overbid), the player who initiated the auction now still claims one item as a consolation prize. Since the initiator must always bid at least one worker, this guarantees that a full jam can no longer leave the player who opened the card with nothing. (It is a floor on full jams only — if a rival's bid survives the overbid, they clinch and the initiator can still take nothing.) Simulation shows the rule is inert at 2 players (full jams essentially never happen with two capped bidders) but a real economic lever at 3–4 players: it rescues 1–2 dead auctions per game, sharply cuts wasted icons, and lifts builds per player. (Confirmed in simulation and applied across the web prototype, the simulator, and the BGA port.)

Card changes before → after

  • Channel Clearer, reined in. Old: “At the start of your turn, you may discard 1 Reed worker from any river card…” — free, and (on BGA) usable as many times as there were targets. New: “At the start of your turn, you may pay 1 fish to discard 1 worker from any Reeds river card (not a wild card); it returns to that player’s supply without a blank.” Two changes: it now costs 1 fish and fires once per turn. That harmonizes it with the beaver’s Tail Slap (the game’s other start-of-turn ability, already 1🐟 and once per turn). The wording also nails down a corner case: a wild card (Driftwood Tangle, the Logs/Reeds wild) is not a valid target — wild cards carry two equal materials and never count as an exact-material card, the same rule Confluence already follows. Finally, with the ability tamed the card was left slightly under-powered at 0★, so it now prints 1★ — simulation puts a 1-VP Channel Clearer dead-center at both 3- and 4-player (a muskrat forced onto it wins within half a point of its fair share), which leaves Stone Tool as the only active-ability starter still worth zero. (Notably 2★, matching Tail Slap, would overshoot at 3-player — the worker-denial effect is a touch stronger than Tail Slap’s blank drop, so it needs less VP.) In simulation the fixed card lands inside the balance band at every player count. (Balance-checked in simulation at 3–4 players; applied consistently across the physical cards, the web prototype, the simulator, and the BGA port.)
  • Wood Pile, wording tightened. Same wildcard clean-up, applied the other direction: Wood Pile now reads “claim 1 uncovered Log icon from any non-wild river card for 1🐟.” It always excluded wild cards in spirit; the card and code now say so outright, so the Logs/Reeds wild (Driftwood Tangle) is never a Wood Pile target. No change to how the card plays — just the same “wilds are never an exact-material card” rule made explicit everywhere it’s tested.

Version v0.2 (July 2026)

A polish pass driven by The Game Crafter Sanity Test (77/100, reviewed by Chris Aylott) — sharpening the rulebook and components, plus one card rework that retires a trap ability.

Why: the sanity review praised the theme and presentation but flagged concrete fixes — a sideways component picture, a rulebook that wants a table of contents and a summary-of-play up front, and a few spots that could use a diagram. v0.2 works through that list.

Components & rulebook

  • Auction chit picture fixed. The chit’s river-scene art was showing sideways in the component list; it now displays upright and is sized to scale next to the material card.
  • Labeled card-anatomy diagrams. The rulebook now calls out the parts of a structure card (title, VP block, cost row, effect text) and a material card (name, material & icon count, item icons, player-count badge, effect), each with numbered markers and a key.
  • Icon legend added to the Quick reference — the four worker chits and the six material icons, named.
  • Every component is now pictured. Added the two that had text only — the fish-track pawn (front and its “+60” flip side) and the blank chit.
  • “How the game plays” overview. A short summary of the flow — fish-track turn order, one action a turn, spend materials to build for VP, finish-line ending — now sits right before the detailed rules, so readers are oriented before the specifics.
  • Thematic cover intro. The cover’s mechanical tagline is replaced with a purely thematic blurb — four riverbank builders racing to raise the grandest works — with no game terms.
  • Full setup diagram. A new “Your table at the start” illustration shows the whole laid-out table — material deck and three revealed Headwaters cards, an empty river, the structure deck with all pawns stacked on space 0, and each player’s workers, hand, and drafted starter — every component keyed to a legend.
  • Endgame section made prominent. It now starts on its own page under a distinctive 🏁 banner header, so tired players can find the end-of-game rules at a glance.
  • A visual play example. A three-step “worked auction” — a card comes up, secret bids are revealed, then everyone pays and places while the card drifts downstream — now illustrates the core loop in pictures.
  • Table of contents & page numbers. A short Contents box on the first page, and a page number on every page, make sections easy to find. Cross-references now point to real page numbers instead of “at the back” / “next page.”
  • Glossary. A short alphabetical glossary of the game’s terms (Headwaters, graduate, jam, tableau, …) near the back.
  • Consistent wording. One term per thing now: the fish-track piece is always the pawn, river cards slide, and the pieces you bid with are worker tokens.

Card changes before → after

  • Tow Line, redesigned. Old: “As an action: pay 2🐟 to slide a river card 1 space toward the Headwaters.” New: “Once per game (flip card): move any river card to River 1, then run an auction on it (no flat 🐟).” The old ability was a trap — sliding a card upstream made it cheaper for whoever auctioned it next, including your rivals, so a careful player never used it. The new once-per-game version keeps the discount for yourself: you yank any drifted river card all the way back to the cheap River 1 rate and immediately auction it, skipping the flat fish. Same 8★ card and cost; the effect is now worth using. (Balance-checked in simulation at 2–4 players.)

Version v0.1.9 (June 2026)

A new endgame, a fresh batch of card tweaks, and one new structure. The headline is the endgame overhaul — the rest follows from it.

The endgame, reworked the big change

Why: in the 4-player playtest the game felt like it should wrap up about when the material deck emptied — but the old snap-to-retirement ending dragged on past that point. The new finish line gives players a clear target to race toward and a clean wind-down.

Before. The endgame began when the material deck emptied: all pawns snapped to spaces 1, 2, 3…, then play continued until each pawn reached space 59 (or passed) and retired. The fish track also punished over-spending with lapping — sprint a full lap past another pawn and you flipped over, "exhausted" and out of turns until they caught up.

After. The game now ends on a finish line, not on the deck:

  • The line is a single flat 90 fish — the same at every player count, sitting on the second lap (the "+60" side, space 30). The game ends by pawns crossing it rather than by the board running dry, and it lands a game at roughly 30 min (2P) / 40 (3P) / 55 (4P).
  • The deck running out does not end the game — you keep auctioning the river and Headwaters cards already on the board until pawns reach the line. But once the material deck is empty, the active player drifts +1 fish at the end of each turn, so the finish line rushes up and the dead-board tail can't drag.
  • The instant your pawn reaches the line, you retire. Once the first player crosses, everyone else may Retire on their turn instead of acting, sliding to the lowest open space at or past the line. No two pawns share a finish space: if you land where a retired pawn already sits, slide forward to the next open space.
  • When all have retired, each player makes one last build, then you score. Ties break to the lower final fish position.
  • Lapping / exhaustion is gone. Instead, flip your pawn chit to its "+60" side each time you pass 0, so your true track position stays readable across laps.
  • Invent now costs at least 2🐟 (choose N = 2–5, was 1–5). A player with nothing to do advances to the finish line faster, which cuts the slow "draw one, inch forward" grind at the end without changing how much anyone builds.

New card — Confluence

Confluence (Reeds 2 / Stones 2 · time 3 · 5★) — As an action: pay 🐟 to run a single auction over two material cards with the same item type (Headwaters or river), pooling all their uncovered icons into one larger pool; both cards then float downriver. A way to force a big, contested grab.

New card — Bramble Shoal 2-player only

Bramble Shoal (Stones / Vines ×5) — a shared wildcard where each claimed icon yields Stones or Vines, chosen at build time. It appears only in 2-player games: at that count the separate Stones-5 and Vines-5 cards (Rocky Shoal and Trailing Vine) come out of the deck and this single card takes their place, adding contention at the count where the material market otherwise thins out and completing the set of three wildcard pairs (logs/reeds, clay/mud, stones/vines). At 3+ players the deck is unchanged.

Card changes before → after

The new endgame caps laps (you pass 0 at most once or twice), which gutted the "At 0 on 🐟 track" cards — they could fire only at the very end. They've been reworked into once-per-game abilities; Mill Wheel's old clamp would have blocked you from finishing, so it became a copy-your-neighbor card.

A few cards were also renamed to stop their titles colliding with the four species names — the beaver hand-size starter is now Beaver Cache (it stacks with the main-deck Cache Burrow), and several generic structures shed their animal prefixes. Cosmetic only; no rules change.

Components

  • Pawns. Each species now has a dedicated fish-track pawn — a chit with a "+60" lap back (flip it when you pass 0). It's used only on the fish track, not as a worker.
  • Blank chits: 41 (up from 28), to cover the 90th-percentile of a busy game.
  • New auction chit. Place it on the card up for auction so everyone can see what they're bidding on.
  • Per-species structure-card backs (drawn from each species' worker icon).
